Image Resizer

Set exact pixel dimensions or pick a preset, lock the aspect ratio if you want, and download the result — all locally.

Resize, resample, or crop — they are not the same thing

People use "resize" for three different operations, and the tool that guesses wrong ruins the image:

  • Stretch forces the image into the new box regardless of shape. Fast, and almost always wrong — faces get wider and circles become ellipses.
  • Fit inside (contain) scales the whole image so it fits, adding empty space where the shape does not match. Nothing is lost, but you get letterboxing.
  • Crop to fill (cover) scales until the box is covered, then trims the overflow. The result fills the frame edge to edge with nothing distorted, at the cost of losing the outer parts of the image.

Cover is what social platforms actually do to your upload, so choosing it here lets you see the crop before publishing rather than after.

The presets, and why those numbers

  • 1200×630 — the Open Graph standard. This is the preview card shown when a link is shared on Facebook, LinkedIn, Slack or Discord.
  • 1080×1080 — Instagram's square post, still the safest crop because the feed can display it without further trimming.
  • 1080×1920 — Stories, Reels and TikTok. Vertical and full-screen.
  • 1280×720 — YouTube thumbnails, and HD video frames.
  • 1500×500 — the X/Twitter profile header, which crops aggressively on mobile.

Upscaling does not add detail

If your source is 800 pixels wide and you ask for 2400, the browser interpolates: it invents pixels by blending their neighbours. The result looks softer than the original, never sharper. A small image cannot be made large and detailed by resizing — if you need more resolution, you need a better source or a purpose-built upscaler.

Downscaling quality

Shrinking is the direction that can genuinely be improved. This tool enables the browser's high-quality smoothing, which averages neighbouring pixels instead of sampling one of them. That is the difference between a clean reduction and one with jagged edges on fine detail — the reason a 4000-pixel photo reduced to 400 can look either crisp or noisy depending on the algorithm.

FAQ

Are my images uploaded?

No. Everything runs on a canvas in this tab.

What is the largest size I can output?

Browsers cap canvas dimensions — commonly 16,384 pixels on a side, and a total area limit that varies. Asking for more than that will fail or produce a blank image.

Why does my transparent PNG have a white background?

You selected JPEG, which has no alpha channel, so transparency is filled with white. Choose PNG or WebP to keep it.

Can I resize many images to the same size at once?

Yes — select multiple files, set the dimensions once, and press Resize. All of them use identical settings, which is what you want for a consistent gallery or product listing.
